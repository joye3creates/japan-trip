## Decisions

### Hers

**"Merge it."** Twice, once for each PR, after seeing the built file locally
first. The preview build is used as a last look before merging rather than as a
way to iterate, which is what keeps the build count at two per publish.

**"Is what we building qualify as vibecoding?"** Not a build decision, but worth
recording: the answer that held up was that the mechanics are vibecoding and the
practice is not, because the part vibecoding usually skips — rejecting the
output with reasons — is the part she does most.

### The assistant's

**Play the newest recording, not the latest day's.** Yesterday's version asked
the latest day for its video and fell back to the oldest file on record when a
day had none. Writing today's entry would have triggered exactly that: Day 5 has
nothing new to record, so the page would have reverted to the Day 3 video with
no warning. Found before it shipped, by reasoning about what the new entry would
do rather than by seeing it.

**Label the video with the day it came from.** Once the video and the latest day
can differ, "Latest · Day 5 of 16" over a Day 4 recording is a lie. The label
now names the recording's day and says "Latest" only when the two agree.

**Tighten the binary email rule rather than exempt the file.** The privacy check
refused the recording over `d@K.Yv` — six bytes of video payload. An email shape
is about six characters, so random bytes throw one every few hundred kilobytes.
In a binary a hit now has to be twelve characters or more; text files keep the
unrestricted pattern, since that is where a real address would be written.
Rejected: an exception for this file, which would have left the next recording
to hit the same wall.

**Commit the Framer component.** It existed in two places, neither of them the
repo, and the stale URL inside it went unnoticed for two days because of that.
