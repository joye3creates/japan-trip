## Decisions

### Yours

**1. Work in Claude Code rather than developing the brief in regular Claude first.**
You asked directly whether to round-trip. The recommendation was no, on the
grounds that the brief was already complete and the real unknowns were about the
*data*, which only a tool that can open files can resolve. You went with it.

*Vindicated immediately.* The files contained a ₹50,000 reconciliation error and
an entirely unrolled spending category. No prompt refinement would have surfaced
that.

**2. Build with placeholders instead of waiting for complete data.**
The notes, photos and step export were all unavailable. The choice was to stall or
to ship with honest gaps. You chose gaps.

*This is the decision that produced a working artefact in one session.* The
alternative was a planning document and nothing to look at.

**3. Fix the exchange rate at 0.57 rather than use the derived figure.**
The data implied 0.58 — food divided out at 0.5858, in-Japan travel at 0.6135.
You specified 0.57 as the trip average because the rate moved over the sixteen
days.

*Correct call.* The derived figure is an average of averages contaminated by
forex markup. You were there; the spreadsheet was not.

**4. Use real names, not roles.** "Traveller B" everywhere rather than "mom" or the
nickname in the totals tab. Small, but it is the difference between an interface
that feels like a personal artefact and one that feels like a template.

**5. Preserve everything as a case-study archive.**
This changed the repo strategy mid-build. Screenshots and raw dumps had been
gitignored as working clutter; they were un-ignored and kept as evidence.

### The assistant's

**1. Route diagram instead of a tile map.** *Forced, then embraced.*
The publishing sandbox blocks external images, so map tiles cannot load. Rather
than fake a basemap or hand-draw an unconvincing coastline, the map became a
diagram at true geographic positions with a latitude/longitude graticule behind
it. The shape you see is genuinely Japan as traced by your route.

*Rejected alternative:* hand-drawing a simplified Japan silhouette from memory.
High risk of looking amateurish, and only one render check was available.

**2. Transport modes as line styles, not seven colours.** Seven hues on a map
would fail colourblind separation and look chaotic. Weight and dash pattern carry
the mode instead, which also makes it read as a rail diagram — apt for a trip
shaped by JR passes.

**3. Visual direction deliberately away from the reference.** The reel used warm
cream, a serif display face and a terracotta accent. That combination is also the
single most common generic AI-design default. Used instead: cool slate neutrals,
a torii vermillion accent, Zen Kaku Gothic New for display, IBM Plex Sans and Mono
for body and figures.

**4. Flat event log, with entries and legs as separate tables.** A leg has an
origin and destination; an entry has one location. One combined table would null
half the columns on every row. Separate tables make every analytic a group-by, and
the eventual live-capture app writes into the same shape.

**5. Two-bucket step estimator rather than proportional split.** A lot of walking
happens inside places rather than between them. Daily totals will split into
transit steps from walking-leg distance and on-site steps as the remainder. It
self-checks: if transit exceeds the daily total, a leg labelled *walk* was really
a train.

**6. Receipts over itinerary on Day 4.** The plan said Arashiyama and the Sagano
railway; every expense names Uji. Money spent is better evidence than a plan made
in advance. Flagged as an open question rather than silently resolved.

**7. Chart palette validated rather than eyeballed.** Six categorical hues run
through a colourblind-separation checker in both themes. Light mode threw a
contrast warning obliging visible labels or a table view — which is why the
category chart carries direct value labels and why the Ledger tab exists.

## Roadblocks

| # | What | Resolution | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `openpyxl` missing | `pip install` | seconds |
| 2 | `poppler-utils` install 404'd | stale index; `apt-get update` first | one wasted attempt |
| 3 | Playwright expected browser build 1243, container ships 1194 | point `executable_path` at the pre-installed Chromium | ~2 min |
| 4 | Geocoding API blocked by egress proxy (403) | wrote all 48 coordinates from knowledge; each place carries a confidence field | ~5 min |
| 5 | **Map tiles blocked by sandbox CSP** | design pivot to route diagram | the biggest one; arguably improved the result |
| 6 | Google Fonts blocked locally (cert) | cosmetic only, loads fine for viewers | none |
| 7 | **Projection bug shipped into first render** — Mercator y in radians, longitude in degrees, country flattened to a band | scale y by 180/π | caught only by screenshotting |
| 8 | 48 labels collided, then clipped at the right edge | greedy collision avoidance by rank; flip labels inward past 78% width | ~3 min |
| 9 | Repo creation refused — session API is repository-scoped | you created the empty repo manually | one round trip |
| 10 | Push refused 403 — Claude GitHub App not installed | you installed it; push then succeeded | one round trip |
| 11 | Copying your source files into the repo blocked by a provenance check | not worked around; `source/README.md` explains what to add | unresolved by design |

**The pattern worth noting:** six of eleven were environment or platform
constraints rather than anything to do with the actual problem. Two were genuine
bugs in the work. The single most valuable habit was rendering the page and
looking at it before publishing, which caught the one failure that code review
would not have.
