## What got built

### The pipeline underneath (small part of the day)

- **`scrub_photos.py`** — `pics/` → `assets/photos/` plus `data/photo_meta.json`.
  Harvests DateTimeOriginal and GPS *before* stripping, bakes orientation with
  `ImageOps.exif_transpose`, resizes to 1600px, then `strip_app_segments()`
  removes every APPn and COM, because Pillow still writes a JFIF APP0.
- **`privacy_gate.py`** — refuses any image metadata at all rather than a list of
  known-bad tags, and gained `--selftest` (7 synthetic containers, 4 must be
  refused, 3 must pass). Late in the day it also learned to decode `data:` URIs
  and put inlined images through the same door.
- **`data/photo_attach.json`** — hand-edited. Says where a photograph belongs
  when the file could not say, and records the two that nothing can place.
- **`scrub_inline.py`** — the same stripper, over images pasted into a page.

### The card

`clock.template.html` gained a 266px card: a 4:3 well with the image contained
and never cropped, place, item, hour, price, a note line, and a foot. Fixed
height at every mark. Hover shows it, click pins it, the dock becomes a stepper,
arrow keys walk the day and the photographs, escape releases.

### The studies, published

`explorations/` with four pages, served at `/explore/`. Three are dead ends kept
on purpose.

## Decisions

### Hers

**A photo belongs to a place, not to a minute.** Decided after asking what the
files knew about themselves. Rejected: matching a photo to the nearest point in
time, which would have put the crater at Mt Aso on an ice cream — the photo is
at 12:50, the ice cream at 11:52 and the lunch at 13:50.

**Threw out three of five designs in one message**, each with a reason: the
filmstrip rail and the day drawer because "they are essentially hidden", the
inline bloom because "when I'm hovering over it, it's flashing". The last one
was a real bug — expanding the row moved the point out from under the cursor,
which closed it again.

**"Or any identifiers."** Three words that turned the image check from a list of
known-bad tags into a refusal of anything that is not pixel data. Correct: the
phone had written its model name into two private vendor tags as JSON, so any
list would have missed it.

**"I actually don't want any movement or any disturbances to happen within the
visualization."** Stated once, then enforced four separate times across the day:
against the bloom, against the arrows appearing on click, against the marks
bobbing while stepping, against the bar resizing.

**Fixed picture size, 4:3, image contained.** Rejected: cropping to fill, which
was what made the first version unusable — "the images are getting cropped".

**Top half opens downward, bottom half upward.** Her observation, unprompted,
before it was a problem.

**Reach 72, strength 10.** Set by moving two sliders in a study rather than by
being asked to imagine a number.

### The assistant's

**Label the stand-ins on the picture itself.** She asked for a placeholder image
on every mark and said to leave the text alone. A real photograph of somewhere
else, shown silently against a purchase, is the same lie as an estimated price,
and the page argues against that in three places. The label sits on the picture
rather than in the text, so the layout she approved is untouched. Rejected:
doing exactly as asked and saying nothing.

**Two transforms per mark.** The outer carries the mark to its position over
0.92s; the inner carries the cursor nudge over 0.16s. On a shared transform the
nudge inherits the travel's easing and arrives after the cursor has gone.

**Rotate the same file rather than ship a second one.** 81 food marks gained a
second picture for zero bytes, and it is unmistakably a placeholder.

**Freeze the spread once a mark is pinned.** The card is covering that stretch of
chart and the arrows are walking the row; motion underfoot at that moment is
noise. Rejected: keeping it live, which would have meant the card chasing its own
mark.

**Refuse bad photo attachments at build time.** An unknown point, an unknown
place, or a place on a day that did not go there. Each one fails silently
otherwise: the picture just never appears.

**Show the risk instead of describing it.** Put real coordinates into a copy of
one of her photos and ran the check on it. It came back clean. One run made the
case for rewriting it; a paragraph would not have.

Set the day, and note "placeholders" — the photos were never the point.

> "Or any identifiers"

Three words, sent alone. The whole safety rule for images, in one line.

> "I'm not happy with the interaction and animation it looks very generic and outdated… the fin strip uh, rail and the day drawer these things I'm not really looking for because they are essentially hidden… I don't understand the inline bloom, like the interaction is really bad when I'm hovering over it, it's flashing… I actually don't want any movement or any disturbances to happen within the visualization… let's treat it as an overview and not complicate it too much"

One message, five separate judgements, each with a reason. Produced the shape of
everything built afterwards. The most useful brief of the day.

> "once I click, then the arrows appear. And it's kind of weird so I don't want that disturbance to happen… for the top five or top half of the rows the images kind of you know appear below the dot and for the bottom ones the images appear on top… The image should be like the hero pretty much."

Three specifications in three sentences, all of them implementable as written.
"The image should be like the hero" set the 4:3 well as the largest element.

> "dont put anything on netlify before i saw so as it takes credits"

A cost instruction that changed the workflow permanently. Netlify bills per
build; a pull request is what spends one, and every push to an open PR spends
another. Branch pushes with no PR build nothing. Already written into CLAUDE.md
the day before; this is the day it started being followed properly.

> "I can't see any image on these right now. So have you added an image or what's the scene?"

The single most valuable sentence of the day. Everything worked; almost nothing
was attached. Three silent failure modes underneath.

> "let's associate one picture with one category as a placeholder for now. So for every food related uh, category, put the Totoro one picture… Essentially, on hovering, over any icon, I should be able to see a picture and uh, the text just keep it like as it is for now, but the images should be placeholder."

Precise and complete: five categories, five pictures, explicitly labelled as a
temporary measure. "The text just keep it like as it is" is what made it safe to
add the placeholder marker without contradicting her.

She had worked out the sizing constraint herself, including which label is the

Asked for a study rather than an implementation, and asked for alternatives

A test fixture, specified exactly, including why: so it is obvious it is a
