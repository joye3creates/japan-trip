## Decisions

### Yours

**1. Pivot the axis from price to time.** The decision of the day, and it changes
what the project is. Price is an accounting question. Time is the travel
question, and almost no travel tool answers it.

*The data said no, at first.* Only 15% of entries carried any time signal, and a
third of them have no moment in principle. You overrode that by pointing out the
notes will carry times, free activities and journey durations, which is the one
thing that makes it viable.

*Rejected by implication:* keeping price as the primary story. It still exists,
one click away, which is the right place for it.

**2. One lodging icon per arrival, not check-in and check-out.** I had proposed
check-in and check-out; you corrected it to an icon every time you arrive at the
lodging, which means two on the first day and one on every night after.

*Yours is simply better.* Mine leaves the middle nights of a multi-night stay
with no lodging mark at all, and four of the seven bookings run two or three
nights. Recorded in `INTERACTION.md` in your formulation.

**3. Pass journeys collapse into the pass mark rather than fading.** Offered
three options; you took the most expensive to build and were right to.

*Why it matters beyond the animation:* it gave the whole interaction one rule
instead of two special cases. A mark either moves or merges into a parent. Your
hotel merge and the pass collapse became the same idea, and that single sentence
is now the spine of the model.

**4. Remove shopping and gifts entirely.** Not hidden in the page, excluded at
build time, so they are not sitting in the source for anyone to read.

*Consequence you may not have intended:* it cost the piece its previous ending.
The old closing beat was about two shopping evenings that no longer exist. What
replaced it is better, because the rail pass turned out to be the real hidden
cost.

**5. A standing privacy rule, not a one-off.** "Keep on flagging these personal
information things." That phrasing is what turned it from a task into a
convention with audit commands attached.

**6. Build with invented times rather than wait two days.** The same call as
session 01's placeholders, and it worked the same way: an artefact to react to
beats a week of specification.

### The assistant's

**1. Icons carry category, colour does not.** Seven categories would need a
seven-hue validated palette that would fight the muted woodblock ground. Your
sketch already drew icons rather than colour, so shape carries identity and the
palette stays as one accent.

**2. A logarithmic price axis, which was not optional.** Purchases run from ¥100
to ¥36,000. On a linear axis 89% of marks land in the leftmost tenth of the
width; on a log axis, 5%. Without this the ledger view does not function at all.

**3. A band above the lanes rather than a corner panel.** You proposed hover
icons in a corner. The numbers argued otherwise: flights and passes are 38% of
the trip, and 38% does not belong behind a mouse gesture that phones do not
have. The band sits on the same axis so the comparison is immediate.

**4. The three-kind mark model.** Both, event, cost. This is the piece of design
I am most pleased with, because it was not asked for. It emerged from trying to
make your hotel rule and the pass collapse work without two separate
implementations, and it turned out to describe the flights too.

**5. Unpriced things get a gutter, not a zero.** The one night with no recorded
price sits left of the axis under a mark reading "n/r". Zero would be a lie, and
a log axis has no zero anyway.

**6. The name mapping lives outside version control.** Replacing names in the
data was not enough, because the replacement rule itself contained them. Moving
it to an untracked file means the repository never holds them at all.

### On roadblock 19, which is the one worth keeping

Asked for hotel bookings, I ran text extraction over the PDF, found three
confirmations, and reported the rest as missing. The user re-sent the same file
twice. On the third attempt they sent a screenshot of a booking and asked
whether I could see it in the PDF.

Only then did I check per page. Page three had **zero characters of text and one
image**. Four pages were rendered images, and every one held a booking.

**The failure was concluding that "no text" meant "no content."** Text
extraction returning something is not evidence it returned everything. The fix
is a two-line check for pages with no text and non-zero images, and it should
run before any claim that a document lacks something.

The user was right and persisted through two wrong answers to prove it. That is
a good outcome but it cost them three messages it should not have.

## Prompts and briefs

**On the axis pivot**

> "my idea is that instead of having the price on the x-axis, what if we keep it
> the time? Like from twelve a.m. till you know twelve um, eleven fifty-nine p.m.
> ... the day can be divided into uh, four quarters. For simplicity"

*Produced:* the whole direction of the project. The quarters became two-hour
bands after I showed that the 00–06 quarter is empty by construction and three
usable buckets make every day look alike. *Note the pattern:* the instinct was
right, the granularity was negotiable, and separating the two made the
conversation quick.

**On the hotel problem**

> "so for hotel, what we do is we put two icons in the day. Um, or like when we
> checked in, that would be one icon ... so how it would look on the chart is uh,
> hotel icon, then uh, food icon, then a train icon, then a food icon, then a
> train icon, and then a hotel icon"

*Produced:* the solution to the objection I had raised, and the seed of the merge
grammar. Describing it as a sequence of icons rather than as a rule is what made
it concrete enough to build from.

**On the pass collapse**

> "I'm not able to understand between two and three ... instead of fading, I
> definitely want it to like be an animation where it's going towards the pass
> mark or you know collapsing into a pass mark thing. Uh, so people become
> curious and maybe they hover and get to know what's happening"

*Produced:* the merge rule. *Worth recording:* saying "I don't understand the
difference" rather than picking one is what got a proper explanation and then a
confident choice. The reason given, wanting people curious enough to hover, is
also a better justification than either option's technical merits.

**On privacy**

> "keep on flagging these personal information things. Make sure that nothing
> personal including our names or email ID or phone number or credit card number
> or any payment ID receipts um goes online anywhere"

*Produced:* the audit, the removal of names from eleven files and three published
pages, and a standing rule in `CLAUDE.md`. *The phrase that did the work* was
"keep on flagging", which asked for a convention rather than a task.

**On the PDF**

> "Aren't you able to see this within the PDF?"

*Produced:* the discovery of four image pages and five missing bookings. Six
words, and they were worth more than anything else said that hour.

**On building before the data exists**

> "Yes, please. Could you build the interaction so we can work on that? Because
> the notes I'll be able to supply you with it after one or two days."

*Produced:* this session's main artefact. The same decision as session 01, made
faster because the first one had paid off.
