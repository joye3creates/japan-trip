Photographs on the Clock

Five ways a photograph can live on the clock

Two real marks, two real photographs, five different interaction models. Low fidelity on purpose: the question is how it behaves, not how it looks.

Everything here works on tap as well as hover, because most people will open this on a phone.

What the data already gives us. Every mark carries place_id and a note field — 163 of 182 have a place, 33 already have notes. So a photograph attaches to a place on a day, which is why Mt Aso works automatically: the photo and six of that day's marks share mt-aso. Timestamps alone would have put a crater photograph on an ice cream.

The two placeholders. mt-aso.jpg knew it was taken at 12:50 on 21 November and placed itself on day 6. totoro-puffs.jpg knew nothing and was assigned by hand to e5, "totori puffs and cookies". Marks with a photograph are filled; the rest are hollow.
01

Hover card

What you asked for. The card carries place, item, time, price and the note. Tap a filled mark to open the side panel, where several photographs for one place become a carousel.

Trade-off: the photo is small and only one person can see it at a time. Good for scanning, weak for looking.

HOVER OR TAP A MARK · TAP A FILLED ONE FOR THE PANEL

02

Filmstrip rail

Photographs get their own row beneath the clock. Hovering a thumbnail lights its mark; hovering a mark lights its thumbnail. The link runs both ways.

Trade-off: photographs stay browsable as photographs, but they leave the timeline, so you lose the sense of when.

HOVER EITHER SIDE · THE OTHER RESPONDS

03

Inline bloom

No overlay at all. The mark grows into the photograph where it stands, and the row opens to make room. Nothing covers the data.

Trade-off: the most tactile of the five, and the only one that never hides the clock. But it moves the rows, which is disorienting if several open at once.

HOVER A FILLED MARK · THE ROW OPENS

04

Day drawer

The clock becomes an index. Click a day label and that day unfolds below it as a strip of its own marks, photographs inline, captions under each.

Trade-off: by far the best for a day with many photographs, and it gives captions real room. But it is a click rather than a glance, so nobody discovers it by accident.

CLICK A DAY LABEL ON THE LEFT

05

Place spotlight

Hovering a mark dims everything that did not happen at that place, across every day, and its photographs stack in the corner. It answers "what was Mt Aso like" rather than "what happened at 12:50".

Trade-off: the only one that shows a place across the whole trip. But it takes over the view, so it suits a deliberate mode rather than an idle hover.
