Stepping through the clock

Hover to look, click to lock, then move along. The card is one fixed size at every point — same block, same square picture well, same reserved room for the controls, so nothing reflows when the arrows appear.

Settled: fixed bar and a 4:3 well. The bar sits at the bottom of the window for now; moving it up under the chart is parked.

Rows 1 to 8 open downward, rows 9 to 16 open upward. Always the same, never decided by whatever space happens to be free.

Two levels of arrows. The ones over the picture move between photographs at that point. The ones below move to the next point in the day. Arrow keys do the same: ← → for points, ↑ ↓ for photographs. Escape releases.

Every point has between one and four photographs, drawn from your six. Only two attachments are real and keep the vermillion ring; the rest are randomly assigned for testing and say so.

The picture is contained, never cropped. Five of your six are portrait and one is landscape, so a square well leaves margins on some. Try the 4:3 well to see the trade: less letterboxing on the landscape, more cropping pressure on the portraits.
